# Public Digital Ore and Truth Substrate review

Read-only inspection on 2026-10-26. The user-provided `/digitalore` path returned a branded 404. The site’s own navigation linked to the working route, [`/digital-ore`](https://universaltruth.life/digital-ore).

That page describes Digital Ore as a local, deterministic intake for text typed by the operator. It exposes local `REFINE & MINT`, `EXPORT`, and `PURGE` controls. It labels the digest `FNV-1a` and shows an eight-hex-character output. Its page says there is “no payout rail” and no third-party signer or escrow. The manifesto asserts rights and a future 0.1% data dividend, but the page does not establish a legally enforceable entitlement, payment mechanism, identity proof, or independent attestation. Those are the site’s claims, not independently verified facts.

The site’s [`Truth Substrate`](https://universaltruth.life/sudo-coin) page calls its mirror `FNV-1a` and describes the output as a sovereign attestation or hashed testimony. During this read-only visit the live readout displayed “Failed to fetch” and had no public-network sample. The page says its inputs are public Bitcoin network readings, but the observed page did not provide a machine-readable feed contract or an independently signed certificate.

Neither inspected page documented a versioned JSON schema, cryptographic signature contract, public API for Truth-Talk, or a Truth-Talk integration. The Digital Ore page presents an export control, but this review did not click it or inspect a generated file; no export format is therefore asserted here. These observations do not prove that no private interface exists elsewhere.

The page’s eight-character FNV-1a display is a local digest, not a digital signature or trusted timestamp. By itself it cannot prove who authored the text, who controlled the browser, when the text existed, who owns the rights, or that a payment is owed. Any future transfer to Truth-Talk must therefore be a user-selected, versioned, operator-approved signed export/import contract, with cryptographic digests and verifiable signatures. This read-only inspection created no connection, fetched no private records, changed no data, and did not modify the separate VALKYRIE codebase.
