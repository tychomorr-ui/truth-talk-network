# Manus hosting terms for user-generated content

Research checked 25 October 2026. This note addresses official Manus rules and hosting documentation relevant to a proposed public feed and short-video surface (“4Reels”), including signed posts and user-uploaded screenshots, recordings, documents, videos, and files. It is not a legal determination.

## What Manus’s published rules say

Manus’s current individual Terms of Use (last updated 28 September 2026) expressly cover tools for creating, deploying, or operating digital projects, and describe creator-operated “Spaces”: public-facing websites, apps, databases, digital services, and other projects that may be published and hosted on a Manus subdomain or custom domain. For a Space, Manus characterizes itself as a platform service provider, not the Space operator or publisher; the Space owner is the independent provider/controller and is responsible for the Space, its content, and activities conducted through it. The owner must provide their own end-user privacy, cookie, terms-of-use and, where needed, refund policies; handle end-user inquiries and complaints; and ensure legal compliance and third-party rights. [1]

The Terms’ content provisions expressly encompass text, software, music, sound, photos, graphics, video, messages, tags, and other materials shared or uploaded. Uploaders are responsible for the content, its accuracy and legality, and obtaining rights, permissions, notices and consents needed for the content and its processing. The user grants Manus and affiliates a broad, worldwide, royalty-free, transferable and sublicensable license to copy, display, upload, perform, distribute, store, modify and otherwise use “Your Content” to provide, maintain, develop and improve the Services and monitor compliance. Manus disclaims ownership, but non-ownership does not mean Manus receives no license. [1]

The linked Usage Policy applies to websites, hosted computing environments, and tools for deploying or operating projects, as well as “all content” and activities carried out using the Service. Material restrictions include unlawful material; infringement or misappropriation; and making available content containing an identifiable person or personal characteristics without that person’s express consent. It also prohibits specified harmful content and use that deceives or misleads others, expressly including furtherance of misinformation or disinformation, and impersonation, unauthorized recording, fraud, privacy-rights violations, and certain high-risk personal-data uses. Manus’s Terms and Safety & Content Policy also restrict use to adults age 18 or older. The Terms specifically say recording, streaming or transcription is allowed only where the user is entitled to capture and process the relevant voice, image, screen, system audio or other content, and requires notices and participant consent where applicable. These are Manus’s published platform rules, not an assurance that a proposed product’s own identity checks or controls satisfy them. [2] [3] [1]

The Terms say Manus may suspend accounts and temporarily or permanently ban a Space it decides is illegal or violates the Usage Policy. Manus may remove Space Content upon a third-party infringement claim, and may delete or disable content it believes violates the Terms or is alleged to infringe. Its Safety & Content Policy describes automated detection for certain illegal categories (including CSEA), action after Manus becomes aware of illegal content, removal of reported harmful content where appropriate, account/feature restrictions, and human review where appropriate for reports and escalations. It provides reporting and appeal channels. The policy does not specify universal proactive review of every public post/file, guaranteed detection, a removal deadline, a content-appeal service level, or coverage of independent Nostr relays and clients. [1] [3]

The current Manus Privacy Policy treats uploaded/generated content broadly: messages, photos, images, audio, music, videos, comments, questions, files and other user-generated content, plus associated metadata, may be processed. It says Space owners/API customers determine processing of their end users’ personal information; Manus acts as processor on the owner’s documented instructions and the DPA. The owner’s privacy policy governs that end-user data. The Privacy Policy says account deletion generally removes information from Manus systems within 30 days, subject to listed legal, safety, security and other retention exceptions. For Space end-user personal information, the owner determines the retention period and Manus retains it on the owner’s instructions. It separately warns that content made public can be seen, collected, cached, copied, screen-captured or stored elsewhere by others, and says Manus is not responsible for others’ use of it. This is not a promise to erase public copies or decentralized replicas. [4]

Official Website Builder documentation describes Manus publishing as deploying an application to a managed hosting environment. Its Cloud Infrastructure page says apps can have a backend, persistent database and file storage, and explicitly identifies user-uploaded images, downloadable PDFs and video files as storage uses. Publishing documentation describes managed Google Cloud Run hosting modes. These documents establish that Manus offers server/database/file-storage capabilities, not that they are enabled for this project or approved here. They do not document a Nostr-specific hosting/moderation contract or guarantee. Keep the proposed implementation static and do not turn on Manus backend, database, file storage, or upload-serving features without separate approval. [5] [6]

## Evidence, identity and control: what each mechanism proves

A SHA-256 digest is a compact fingerprint of bytes. Under the hash standard, digests can be used to detect whether a message changed after its digest was generated. A matching digest therefore supports the narrow claim “these bytes match the bytes represented by this digest,” assuming the digest itself is authentic and the comparison is performed correctly. A bare hash does not establish who created or uploaded the file, when it existed, whether it is genuine or unedited before hashing, whether its caption is true, whether depicted people consented, or whether the file is lawful. A hash-anchored receipt adds evidence that a particular digest was included in a particular anchor/event; it does not independently prove the assertions attached to that digest. [7]

In Nostr NIP-01, an event ID is the SHA-256 of a specified serialization of event data, and the event carries a signature over that ID made with the event author’s private key. A correctly validated signature supports that the event was signed by whoever controlled the corresponding private key and that the signed event data has not been altered without invalidating the signature. It does not identify that key-holder as a particular real-world person, show that the key was not stolen or shared, validate the truth of the post, or prove that a claimed recording time is true; `created_at` is an event field, not by itself a trusted timestamp. Avoid presenting “signed” as “identity verified.” [8] [9]

C2PA Content Credentials can cryptographically bind provenance assertions to an asset and make the credential’s assertions tamper-evident. Validation can show that specified assertions/manifests are well-formed, associated with the asset and signed under a credential/trust model. C2PA’s own explainer says it does not judge whether provenance statements are true or whether media is factually accurate, and the core specification does not directly establish the real-world identity of a human or organization. A valid credential is therefore a provenance signal, not a truth badge, universal deepfake detector, consent proof or substitute for identity proofing. [10]

Identity proofing is a separate process: NIST distinguishes IAL (confidence in a claimed real-world identity) from AAL (authentication of an account/session). NIST describes IAL1 as validating core attributes against authoritative or credible sources and linking them to the applicant; higher levels require additional evidence and more rigorous procedures. A platform should state what evidence/checks it actually performs and the scope of the resulting assurance. A verified email, Nostr key, signature, C2PA credential, face match, or badge is not by itself equivalent to a stated real-world identity-proofing level. Even successful proofing does not make a person’s posts or attached media true. [11]

Deletion and moderation are also distinct from proof. A local “hide” or block control changes the client’s display, not the underlying event or bytes held elsewhere. Nostr NIP-09 defines an author-signed deletion request; relays SHOULD delete or stop publishing referenced events, and clients SHOULD hide or label them, but NIP-09 expressly says deletion cannot be guaranteed across all relays and clients. A report, trust label, or moderator decision is a platform action, not proof that the reported claim is false. For public content, design for retention and redistribution beyond the uploader’s control; do not promise universal erasure. [12] [4]

## Implementation constraints for Truth-Talk / 4Reels

- Treat a Manus-published client as a Space if deployed on Manus. Publish the owner’s own audience-facing privacy notice and terms, explain content/publicity and complaint handling, and obtain documented permissions/consents for people identifiable in uploaded material—especially screenshots, screen recordings and video. The Manus Usage Policy’s express-consent rule is unusually direct and should be reflected in product gating and upload guidance. [1] [2]
- Keep the approved architecture static-only: no Manus-provisioned backend, persistent database, or Manus file-storage upload path. Manus documentation describes these as available platform components but does not mean they are pre-approved. If enabled later, treat uploads and end-user personal data as hosted/processed data with explicit retention, access, security, complaint, moderation and deletion ownership; review the applicable Terms, Privacy Policy and DPA for the actual arrangement. [5] [6] [4]
- Do not label an account, post or file “real,” “authentic,” “verified identity,” or “true” solely because it has a SHA-256 receipt, valid Nostr signature, valid C2PA credential, or moderation status. Display the narrower fact and verification basis (e.g., “digest matches,” “signed by this Nostr key,” “C2PA manifest validates,” “identity checked by method X,” or “moderator action applied”). [7] [8] [10] [11]
- Build moderation/reporting and appeal routes for illegal, harmful, impersonating and rights-violating content, plus abuse response for signed posts and attached files. The Manus policy describes its own safety mechanisms; it does not promise to operate as the product’s moderator or cover public material replicated through third-party relays. [1] [3] [8] [12]
- Make retraction truthful: deleting a post from the client, issuing NIP-09, or deleting a Manus account does not guarantee removal from relays, recipients’ caches/downloads, screenshots, archives or other clients. Preserve provenance receipts only with clear disclosure of what remains and avoid storing personal data in an immutable/public receipt unless deliberately intended. [12] [4]
- Enforce the 18+ restriction and avoid any product flow that invites deceptive identity claims, unauthorized recording, or publication of identifiable third parties without express consent. Manus’s published policy prohibits deception and impersonation; no official Manus source reviewed documents a specific anti-catfishing identity verification service or guarantee. [1] [2] [3]

## Material gaps and caveats

The official sources reviewed provide broad Terms, Usage Policy, Safety & Content Policy, Privacy Policy, DPA and website-builder documentation. They do **not** provide a distinct public-feed/UGC/short-video addendum that addresses 4Reels, Nostr relays, relay governance, decentralized storage, viral media, global takedown or a moderation SLA. The Manus terms treat Space content broadly and impose extensive responsibility on the Space owner, but current public evidence does not answer whether Manus would support a particular decentralized UGC service, its capacity/retention terms, or how its own moderation controls would apply to content replicated outside Manus hosting. Do not infer approval, comprehensive moderation, or universal deletion from the general policies.

A separate older privacy statement at `manus-meta.com` identifies a different Manus Technology Group entity and an earlier effective date. This research relies on the current `manus.im` Privacy Policy linked by the current Terms for claims about the Manus individual Services; do not combine the legacy statement’s deletion language with the current policy as though they were one controlling document.
