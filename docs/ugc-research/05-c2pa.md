# C2PA provenance for screenshots and video

**Research checked:** 2026-10-25. The current official technical specification page consulted is C2PA 2.4. This note addresses what provenance can establish for Truth-Talk public-feed uploads and the 4Reels surface; it is not an authenticity guarantee or a policy/legal assessment.

## Core distinction: commitment, signature, provenance, identity

A SHA-256 digest is a content fingerprint/commitment to particular bytes. If the bytes change, the expected digest will ordinarily no longer match; a digest alone does not say who made the file, when or where it was captured, whether it depicts reality, or whether its claimed history is complete. A timestamped, independently verifiable anchor can support the narrower statement that a digest was committed by the anchoring time; it still does not establish that the file was captured as claimed. C2PA itself uses SHA-256 in content bindings and signs the provenance claim, rather than treating an unkeyed hash as authorship evidence. [1] [2]

A Nostr event ID is the SHA-256 digest of a specified serialization of the event, and the event signature signs that ID. Successful NIP-01 verification supports that the event data was signed by the private key corresponding to its included public key. It does not prove that the key belongs to a named human, that a linked file is truthful, or that the signed event’s claims about the file are accurate. A post signature and a media credential are separate statements: an event can identify a file by digest, but that does not itself certify the file’s capture or edit history. [7]

A C2PA Content Credential is a signed manifest of assertions bound to an asset. Validation can establish that the signed claim and its assertions have not been altered and that the manifest’s hard binding matches the covered portions of the asset. A valid hard binding is about the file as signed and later checked; it does not prove the claims were factually true when entered, detect deception already present before signing, or show that every edit/custodian in the asset’s life was recorded. C2PA explicitly does not make value judgments about whether provenance assertions are true or good. [1] [3] [5]

C2PA's standard trust path is to a certificate for a hardware/software signer (a claim generator), under a supported trust list. “Trusted” therefore means trusted under the validator’s trust-list and validation policy—not verified real-world identity of the uploader, camera operator, person depicted, or author of every assertion. Only assertions identified as signer-created in the claim are attributed to that signer; collected/gathered assertions are not. A broken or compromised signing key can also produce technically valid-looking claims attributed to that signer, which makes key protection and certificate status relevant. [1] [2] [3] [9]

## Edit histories, validation and screenshots

C2PA supports successive provenance manifests, action assertions and ingredient links to represent creation, editing and source assets. The history is opt-in and may be incomplete: an edit made in non-C2PA-aware software may not be represented. Adding a new credential later can describe the then-current asset, but cannot reliably reconstruct an undocumented intermediate history. An ingredient’s complete provenance may not be verifiable unless its source data is available. [1] [2] [3]

The 2.4 specification distinguishes **Well-Formed**, **Valid** and **Trusted** manifests. In simplified terms, well-formed means the manifest and its assertions/ingredients meet normative structural requirements; valid adds an intact claim signature, acceptable validity/revocation checks and required signature validation; trusted adds that the signing credential chains to a supported trust anchor. A **Valid asset** further requires the active manifest to be valid or trusted and the portions covered by its content bindings to be unmodified since that manifest. The UI must not collapse these statuses into a single “authentic” badge. The C2PA UX guidance recommends explaining trust-list or validation failures and showing provenance gaps rather than implying certainty. [1] [6]

The official CAI open-source FAQ explicitly says that a screenshot of an image does not include the original image’s C2PA metadata. A screenshot is a new capture/derivative, not proof of the provenance of what was displayed. A C2PA-enabled camera may sign its photograph of a screen, but the credential describes that camera capture and does not automatically identify the displayed image or detect that it was AI-generated. The FAQ says credentials indicate when a file lacks historical data; it does not say a missing credential means an image is fake. [4]

The official material reviewed did not state a distinct normative rule specifically for browser/desktop **screen recordings**. The safe engineering inference is that a recording of rendered pixels is a new video asset and does not inherit the original file’s manifest or hard binding merely by depicting it. A C2PA-aware recorder could credential its own output and declared actions; absent an explicit, verifiable ingredient/source relationship, this would not authenticate the content shown on screen. Do not label screen-recorded material “original,” “camera verified,” or “source verified” solely because the recording itself has a valid credential. [1] [4]

A missing credential means “no credential found/available to this verifier,” not “invalid,” “manipulated,” “AI,” or “catfish.” Credentials may be stripped, may never have been created, or may be inaccessible. C2PA soft bindings such as fingerprinting or watermarks can help rediscover manifests after removal, but this requires matching infrastructure and an accessible manifest repository. C2PA does not require cloud hosting; without an approved server/database, the product should treat external recovery as unavailable unless a third-party repository is explicitly integrated. [1] [2] [4] [5]

## Identity, deletion and moderation

The core C2PA specification does not require or directly establish a human or organizational identity. Its official FAQ says attribution to people/organizations is outside core C2PA; extensions can carry identity assertions. C2PA guidance describes an identity assertion as an additional signed statement about an actor and their relationship to media, not as a guarantee that the asserted real-world identity has been independently proofed. Anti-catfishing therefore needs a separate identity-proofing and account-security mechanism, clearly separated from C2PA provenance status. [2] [3]

C2PA is not DRM and does not prevent use, removal, copying or redistribution of an asset. A content hash can help detect a byte mismatch or match known material, but it does not delete copies or enforce moderation. Nostr NIP-09 defines a signed deletion **request**; relays should delete/stop publishing matching events and clients should hide or indicate them, but the NIP expressly says deletion cannot be guaranteed across all relays and clients. A moderation decision or label is likewise a policy action by a client/relay/operator, not a fact established by the C2PA credential. Public hash anchors can persist after media deletion, so they should be designed as durable commitments rather than represented as a means of erasure. [2] [8]

## Product constraints for a static, decentralized client

- Treat `SHA-256 file digest`, Nostr event signature, C2PA manifest validation, real-world identity proofing and moderation/deletion as distinct indicators. State exactly which object was checked and by which verifier.
- For a file receipt, record at minimum the algorithm and digest, what exact file/byte representation was hashed, and the signed Nostr event that commits to that digest. If claiming “anchored,” identify the anchor and what it proves (at most, commitment/existence by an anchor time under that anchor’s assumptions). Never say the hash alone proves capture, authorship or truth.
- Keep the uploaded bytes intact for credential inspection where possible. Resizing, transcoding, thumbnailing, metadata stripping or video packaging can break the hard binding; if the feed transforms the upload, preserve/verify the original separately and describe transformations as new derived assets rather than silently transferring a “verified” badge.
- Make the validator report at least: credential absent/unavailable; manifest malformed; signature/content binding invalid; valid but not trusted under the selected trust list; trusted manifest; and valid asset binding. Explain “trusted” as a signer/certificate trust result, not a human identity or truth result. Show incomplete history and unavailable ingredient checks.
- Screenshot and screen-recording uploads need a conspicuous “new capture; source not established” state unless the original source is separately identified and verified. Do not infer that absent credentials imply deception or a policy violation.
- A browser-only client can hash files and verify signed data locally if it has a suitably secure implementation and the relevant trust material. But creating **trusted C2PA credentials** is more than hashing: C2PA certificate issuance is tied to a conforming generator product, conformance/security evaluation and a C2PA-trusted CA path. No server is inherently required by the standard, and offline workflows are possible with provisioned keys/certificates, but the project must not claim C2PA conformance or trusted signing until its generator, key custody, certificate, validator and trust/revocation handling have actually been established. [2] [5]
- A no-database/static design should not promise durable-credential recovery, central takedowns, global deletion, identity proofing or dependable abuse moderation unless separately provided. NIP-09 requests do not guarantee erasure; provenance technology does not implement policy enforcement. Decide explicitly how public hashes, manifests and cached media behave after a user requests deletion, since removing the file does not retract an already published commitment. [2] [4] [8]
- Do not market C2PA as “anti-catfishing.” It can add file-provenance signals and may carry optional signed identity assertions; those do not independently prove that the account holder is the real person depicted or claimed. 4Reels must retain separate reporting, safety and moderation controls.

## Evidence gaps and version notes

The current official technical specification consulted was version 2.4. The official FAQ and UX guidance have their own update cadence. The explicit screenshot behavior comes from CAI’s official open-source FAQ rather than a normative sentence in the 2.4 specification. I found no equally explicit primary-source rule for screen-recording provenance; the corresponding limitation above is labeled as an inference from asset binding and the screenshot FAQ. C2PA currently provides no technical verdict on whether depicted content is factually true or whether a user is a catfish. [1] [2] [4] [6]
