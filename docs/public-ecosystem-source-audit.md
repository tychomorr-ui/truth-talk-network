# Public Ecosystem Source Audit and FourReels Integration Roadmap

**Prepared from the fifteen structured audit notes supplied for this task.** No new network research was conducted. Findings below describe only the audit observations in those notes. They do not authenticate the owner-supplied roster, product roles, domains, identities, mesh, or backend systems.

## Executive summary

The fifteen supplied audits range from meaningful inspection of deployed frontend bundles and static pages to checks that stopped at unavailable or restrictive crawl-policy endpoints. The strongest source-level findings concern **TESSERACT-TERMINUS** (`universaltruth.life`), the **distinct Tesseract recovery Mirror** (`tesseract.manus.space`), and **KEIRA** (`keira.nexinus.net`). They expose materially different provenance mechanisms: TESSERACT-TERMINUS includes local records and a client-side signed-message format; the Mirror uses short FNV-1a checksums in in-memory receipts; KEIRA contains a client-side Merkle/Ed25519 verifier whose report supplies its own verification key. These are observable frontend implementations, not proof of authoritative storage, issuer identity, live peer services, or a trusted mesh.

**VALKYRIE** (`valkyrie.nexinus.net`) could not be inspected beyond generic not-found representations at the standard robots and sitemap URLs. No source-level behavior or provenance record was observed. The owner-supplied function “Adjudication / Dark Mirror / VERDEX” is a role label, not an audit finding.

FourReels should integrate only through explicitly approved, versioned, and testable interfaces. For now, keep the existing standalone Truth-Talk boundary intact. There must be **no live VALKYRIE or mesh integration without a versioned, operator-approved, signed export/import contract; no server keys; and all node references must require a user click**. A presentation can frame the aim as “from the trust economy toward a truth economy,” while making clear that no evidence here establishes categorical superiority over TikTok, Nostr, or Reddit.

## Scope, evidence labels, and caveats

This report uses the following distinctions:

- **Observed public frontend/source behavior** means content, routes, or code was reported as visible in a fetched public page or linked deployed frontend asset. It does not mean that code was executed, that a backend was contacted, or that a claimed service works in production.
- **Page claim** means copy, labels, metadata, or displayed values presented by a site. Unless separately stated as observed behavior, it is not independently verified.
- **Owner-supplied role/roster** means a name, function, or endpoint provided in the task roster. It is context, not proof of domain ownership, operator identity, or product capability.
- **Unknown / unverified** includes private backend behavior, persistence and retention, live service health, identities and key ownership, peer status, mesh enrollment, and external integrations not tested.
- Robots and crawl-policy constraints were respected. When policy was unavailable, the supplied audits stopped before entering application routes. A URL that returned a fallback page or an extraction error does not establish the site's actual HTTP status unless the audit expressly recorded it.

Counts in this report refer to the supplied structured notes: **15 audits** total. **Thirteen were partial or blocked**: nine stopped before inspecting application routes, ROOT-GATE exposed only a partial HTML shell, the Mirror had substantial route exclusions, TESSERACT-TERMINUS had two known route exclusions, and KEIRA’s route inventory was incomplete. **Six audits inspected at least some application-route material** (ROOT-GATE’s partial shell, Terminus, Mirror, KEIRA, RESONATE EARTH, and XINUS PORTFOLIO). Two audits—RESONATE EARTH and XINUS PORTFOLIO—completed their stated sitemap scope. No audit failures were reported.

> “Complete” here means complete only within a stated sitemap or permitted-route scope, not verification of backend or site-wide behavior. TESSERACT-TERMINUS inspected all permitted discovered routes but has known exclusions; KEIRA inspected three identified routes but could not establish coverage completeness.

### What the comparison means for provenance

The three inspected frontends expose distinct, limited evidence mechanisms—not interchangeable trust guarantees. Terminus contains client-side content-addressing and Ed25519 message operations alongside local storage and possible configured peer POSTs. The Mirror creates short FNV-1a checksums in an in-memory local-turn receipt, with no exposed signature. KEIRA verifies a report's hashes and an Ed25519 attestation using a public key supplied in that same report, without an independent trust anchor. VALKYRIE's mechanisms are unknown because the frontend was not inspected. None of these observations alone establishes the real-world identity of an operator, an authoritative record store, immutability, a valid mesh, or a live system.

## FourReels integration roadmap

The endpoints below are the **owner-designated endpoints in the supplied roster/audit notes**, not independently authenticated owners or systems. “Corrected” here means keeping the distinct products and their documented evidence boundaries separate; it does not imply that the domains or role assignments have been verified.

### Phase 0 — Boundary and governance (now)

1. Preserve the current **standalone Truth-Talk boundary**: no hidden background connection, shared state, remote inference, or automatic federation with ecosystem endpoints. Do not treat the Mirror's local-only turn path as proof that every related route is local.
2. Maintain an endpoint registry for the designated candidates—ROOT-GATE (`rootgate.manus.space`), TESSERACT-TERMINUS (`universaltruth.life`), the distinct Tesseract Mirror (`tesseract.manus.space`), XINUS-CLARITY (`exonissuite-ceruhtwo.manus.space`), MONARCH-OS (`schedtool-7vbikw4h.manus.space`), TESSERACT-A (`clarityapp-hw9fbadc.manus.space`), KEIRA (`keira.nexinus.net`), VALKYRIE (`valkyrie.nexinus.net`), ARCHANGEL (`archangelcp-sbwmjhce.manus.space`), PROJECT RECLAIM (`reclaimporta-gsio8jvp.manus.space`), IWA SAGE (`iwawellness-khqgjzqe.manus.space`), RESONATE EARTH (`resonate-earth.live`), PROPERTY MAINTAINED (`propmaint-2wv2njwj.manus.space`), NATIVE GROUNDS (`tribalcoff-y9afmafg.manus.space`), and XINUS PORTFOLIO (`nexsens-3uimbss6.manus.space`).** Mark each URL owner-designated and each role unverified until independently authorized.
3. Do not copy, solicit, or embed server-side secrets, signing keys, private keys, tokens, or credentials. Never infer consent from a public frontend or a page link. No keys should be transferred to FourReels.
4. Require written operator approval, a named accountable operator, threat model, data classification, privacy/retention rules, and a rollback plan before any connection. Publicly crawlable is not equivalent to approved for integration.

### Phase 1 — Local, read-only evidence adapters

Implement isolated, opt-in adapters in a sandbox or test fixture using only user-provided/exported sample files. Each adapter should label its source, acquisition time, schema/version, and verification status; preserve the original bytes; and report parse/verification errors without changing Truth-Talk's standalone state. Do not probe peer URLs, execute remote code, submit user content, or invoke undocumented endpoints.

- For **TESSERACT-TERMINUS**, model its observed local PAM fields and CID/signature message envelope separately. Distinguish `CID computed`, `signature cryptographically valid`, `key pinned/trusted`, and `remote receipt confirmed` as different statuses. Do not claim a PAM signature implementation or authoritative remote ledger from this audit.
- For the **Tesseract Mirror**, treat its FNV-1a values as short, non-cryptographic checksums only. Never map them to cryptographic hashes or signatures. Local receipts must remain visibly local and ephemeral unless the user explicitly exports them.
- For **KEIRA**, verify the specified canonicalization/Merkle/Ed25519 rules only on a locally supplied report. Label the result “valid under supplied report key” unless an independently authenticated trust-anchor mechanism is approved. Include excluded fields (`generatedAt`, `trajectory.queryHash`) in the UI's explanation of what is and is not covered.
- For **VALKYRIE**, permit no adapter or inbound/outbound exchange at this stage: there is no inspected frontend contract to implement safely.
- For all other roster endpoints, keep status `not assessed / no contract observed` unless the operator supplies an approved specification. Absence of inspection is not a negative capability finding.

### Phase 2 — Contract-first, signed export/import (future gate)

A future integration requires an explicit, versioned contract (for example a signed JSON export/import schema with documented canonicalization and test vectors). At minimum it must define schema/version negotiation; issuer and key-discovery/trust-anchor policy; signature algorithm and exact signed bytes; record IDs and provenance chain; timestamps and replay/expiry rules; data sensitivity, consent, retention and deletion; import validation; error handling; revocation/key rotation; and operator support/rollback. Each side must sign off on the contract and test interoperability using synthetic data before real user data is considered.

**Specific hard gate:** no live **VALKYRIE** or **mesh** integration absent a versioned, operator-approved, signed export/import contract. Until then, do not connect to VALKYRIE, mesh manifests, peer health/status endpoints, or configured peer transports. Even after approval, integration should be export/import-based and user-triggered—not ambient, live, or automatic.

### Phase 3 — User-mediated, least-privilege pilot

After the contract passes review, run a small opt-in pilot with test accounts and non-sensitive records. Make every import/export action explicit, preview exactly what will leave or enter, display issuer/key confidence and verification limits, log consent locally, and provide cancel/revoke/delete paths. Use public keys only where needed; keep private keys and server credentials outside FourReels. Rate-limit and isolate parsers; treat imported content as untrusted data, not instructions or executable code.

**Node-reference rule:** every node name, URL, or identifier shown in FourReels must be inert text until the user clicks it. No automatic fetch, prefetch, health probe, DNS resolution, telemetry, link preview, or background navigation. Show the destination before opening it, and do not imply that a listed node is live, verified, or safe solely because it is referenced.

### Phase 4 — Review and expand only on evidence

Review the pilot for privacy leakage, untrusted-key presentation, schema drift, logging, and user comprehension. Keep adapters disabled by default and version-pinned. Expand only when each endpoint has an inspected/approved contract and a separately documented evidence basis. If contract, signing, or operator approval is missing or revoked, disable the adapter and remain standalone.

## Presentation script

> We are building toward a shift **from the trust economy toward a truth economy**: not asking people to trust a label, platform, or authority by default, but helping them inspect evidence, provenance, and uncertainty.
>
> This audit found several different things—not one unified network. TESSERACT-TERMINUS exposes browser-side content identifiers and signed-message code; the separate Tesseract Mirror creates local receipts with short non-cryptographic checksums; KEIRA includes a verifier that can check hashes and a signature against a key included in the report; and VALKYRIE could not be inspected under the available crawl conditions. Those are specific frontend observations, not proof of trusted identities, authoritative storage, a healthy mesh, or working backends.
>
> Truth-Talk remains standalone. We are not claiming that it is categorically better than TikTok, Nostr, or Reddit. Different products serve different purposes, and this audit did not conduct comparative product testing. Our next step is disciplined: use transparent, user-controlled exports; verify what can actually be verified; label what remains unknown; and require an approved, signed, versioned contract before any live ecosystem connection. No server keys, no automatic mesh access, and no node lookup unless the user chooses to click.
